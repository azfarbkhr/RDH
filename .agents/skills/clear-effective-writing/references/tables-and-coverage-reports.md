# Tables and coverage reports

Use for confidence, coverage, or assessment reports. Design the table around the reader's decision. Use the smallest set of columns that each performs a distinct job; remove overlapping assessment, limitation, direction, or priority columns before shortening cell text. Keep a grouping column when it materially improves scanning.

Do not populate cells merely for completeness. Fill gap, risk, or next-action cells only when the row has a material unresolved issue or necessary action. For a high-confidence or sufficiently covered area, leave the gap cell blank unless a specific essential risk remains. Do not invent maintenance work, repeat the coverage statement, or write a variation of `none` to make the table look complete.

Keep rows at a decision-useful activity level. Merge subactivities that support the same assessment, omit categories outside the report's scope, and keep demonstrated coverage separate from future work.
