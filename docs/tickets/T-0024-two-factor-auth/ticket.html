Protect accounts with two-factor authentication

Ticket: T-0024. Delivery role: developer. Relative effort: M.

Lifecycle, priority, dependencies, and ticket path are maintained only in the queue, sourced from tickets.jsonl.

Outcome

Let users enroll in and remove an authenticator-based second factor, reducing account takeover risk.

Current evidence

Verified in apps/api/src/index.ts: authentication is email/password, password hashing uses Node scrypt, and signed-in sessions use a 30-day HTTP-only cookie. There is no 2FA or recovery flow. The owner requested an optional setup control.

Scope and exclusions

Propose optional TOTP with one-time recovery codes, enrollment and disable screens under account settings, and enforcement at login only when enabled. SMS and email OTP, mandatory 2FA, administrator policy controls, SSO, and account-recovery support from human staff are excluded. Security reviewer and Product Owner must approve the recovery and secret-storage design before implementation.

Acceptance checks

IDObservable done-check
AC01Enrollment requires reauthentication, secret confirmation, and a valid one-time code before activation.
AC02Secrets and recovery codes are stored and displayed safely; recovery codes are single-use and shown only once.
AC03Login and sensitive account changes require the second factor when enabled.
AC04Users can rotate recovery codes and disable 2FA only after strong reauthentication; failures are rate-limited.

Research, design, and interfaces

Use the account surface from T-0023. Resolve vetted TOTP implementation, at-rest encryption/key configuration, replay prevention, clock window, attempt throttling, recovery-code entropy and one-time consumption, session policy, and lost-device flow. Never return or log a stored secret after enrollment confirmation. No email requirement is implied; recovery codes are the proposed recovery mechanism.
